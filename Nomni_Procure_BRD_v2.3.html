1. Executive Summary

This document defines the business requirements for Nomni Procure, the rebuilt, AI-native successor to Zeemart. It supersedes the previous "HQ Module Enhancements" BRD and broadens scope from incremental HQ enhancements to a full product repositioning.

The strategic intent is to move Nomni Procure from a procurement tool to the operating system for multi-outlet F&B procurement — combining centralised HQ governance, a full procure-to-pay lifecycle, real-time cost intelligence, and a named-agent AI layer that executes work rather than merely reporting on it. The rebuild follows a Strangler Fig approach: the existing Java/MongoDB backend is preserved behind a headless API layer, while the frontend, analytics, and agentic capabilities are built new.

Two competitors anchor the competitive analysis: Restoke (AI-led restaurant management, strongest in automated insights and invoice intelligence) and Supy (multi-branch inventory and procurement control, strongest in approval workflows, central kitchen operations, and granular permissions). A structured review of the current platform's knowledge base (§4) establishes the baseline: ordering, approvals, GRN receiving, 2/3-way match, invoice digitisation, PEPPOL e-invoicing, and a live supplier product (Nomni Supply) already exist — more than earlier drafts assumed — while HQ governance, agentic AI, unified variance analytics, and the marketplace layer are the genuine gaps.

Nomni Procure must reach parity on competitor table-stakes and win on three differentiators: a transactional supplier network, agentic execution with human-grade guardrails, and HQ governance depth at 100+ outlet scale.

2. Background & Strategic Context

Zeemart established a position in Southeast Asia as a B2B procurement platform connecting F&B buyers with suppliers. The existing HQ Module BRD addressed governance pain for multi-outlet groups. Those requirements remain valid, but the market has moved: the category is consolidating around AI-powered back-of-house platforms, with global players (including Oracle NetSuite Restaurant Operations, launched March 2026) entering the space.

At the same time, AI capability has changed what "good" looks like. Restoke markets itself as an AI restaurant manager whose agentic features handle ordering, prep, and task distribution. Supy positions as the data-driven operating system for multi-branch restaurants. A rebuilt Nomni Procure that simply modernises the UI would launch at parity with 2024; this BRD specifies a product that leads in 2026 and beyond.

2.1 Why now

  • The frontend/UX of the current platform is dated and constrains adoption, particularly for outlet-level users on mobile.
  • The architecture decision is made: headless API layer over the existing Java/MongoDB backend (Strangler Fig), enabling a new AI-native frontend without a backend rewrite.
  • Competitors are converting our addressable market: both Restoke and Supy are active in Singapore and Australia, directly overlapping our core geography.
  • AI-assisted delivery (Claude Code, parallel agent sessions) compresses the rebuild timeline to an estimated 5–6 months, making a leapfrog release commercially viable.

3. Competitive Analysis

This section summarises the two reference competitors and derives the capability bar Nomni Procure must meet or beat. Claims are drawn from public product material as of June 2026 and should be re-validated quarterly.

3.1 Restoke (restoke.ai)

Positioning: "AI-powered restaurant management" — an AI restaurant manager that unifies POS, accounting, suppliers, and team operations. Founded 2020 in Melbourne; 2,000+ venues across AU, NZ, Singapore, the US, and the UK.

  • Restaurant Intelligence: dashboard insights across purchases and sales, live COGS from POS integration, automatic trend and driver detection, multi-venue analytics with deeper HQ and CPK modules.
  • Invoice intelligence: AI/OCR invoice scanning, automatic categorisation and reconciliation, one-click sync to accounting platforms.
  • Agentic operations: AI used as a task manager for ordering, prep, and to-do lists distributed to staff devices; auto-ingests and maps purchasing, scheduling, and sales data on onboarding.
  • Automated procurement: par-level driven ordering — scheduled orders checked against optimal stock levels; any vendor can be connected.
  • Team management: checklists, training modules, task assignment.
  • Claimed outcomes: 15.6% reduction in F&B costs; 10.1% labour cost reduction from automation.
Assessment: Restoke's strength is breadth of automation and the "AI manager" narrative. Its HQ module is comparatively shallow on governance — it centralises visibility more than it enforces control.

3.2 Supy (supy.io)

Positioning: "The operating system for multi-branch restaurants." 3,000+ restaurants in 30+ countries; strong in the Gulf and expanding globally.

  • Full procurement lifecycle: requisitions → multi-level approvals → purchase orders → receiving → invoice reconciliation, on mobile or portal, with live price-variance monitoring and spend limits.
  • Granular control: 200–300+ customisable user permissions, ordering policies, and approval workflows.
  • Inventory depth: real-time stock across sites, live COGS, automated actual-vs-theoretical variance analysis, par levels with auto-replenishment, audited inter-branch stock transfers.
  • Central kitchen: a first-class CK entity — branch ordering portal, demand consolidation, production planning, dispatch/receive confirmation with variance tracking.
  • Menu engineering: recipe costing linked to live ingredient prices and POS sales for real-time profitability analysis.
  • Claimed outcomes: food cost cut up to 25%; waste reduced 80%+; ~65 working days saved per location per year.
Assessment: Supy's strength is operational rigour — approvals, permissions, variance control, and central kitchen workflows. Its AI story is thinner: automations and alerts rather than agents that execute.

5. Data Architecture — Buyer-Primary Item Model

A foundational change to the Nomni Procure data model: the shift from supplier-primary (items exist only if a supplier lists them) to buyer-primary (buyers own a canonical item master; supplier SKUs are fulfillment options linked to it).

5.1 Current model constraints

  • Buyers cannot create items for goods sourced outside the platform.
  • Recipes and inventory can only reference items with an active supplier SKU.
  • Switching supplier for an item breaks its linkage to recipes, inventory, and historical orders.
  • Items produced in-house (central kitchen prep items, own-brand goods) cannot be represented at all.

5.2 Target model

Three distinct layers:

  • Internal Item — buyer-owned, exists independently of any supplier. Anchor for recipes, inventory, par levels, COGS, and reporting.
  • Supplier SKU Link — connects an internal item to one or more supplier catalogue entries, each with its own price, pack size, UOM conversion, and lead time.
  • Preferred Link — per-outlet or per-group designation of the default fulfillment source, with fallback alternatives.

5.5 Standalone → HQ upgrade: reconciliation workflow

StepWhat happens
Step 1 — AI-assisted matchingAutomated matching pass comparing outlet items vs HQ master using name similarity, category, UOM, and supplier SKU signals. Confidence score assigned. ≥85% threshold auto-proposed; below threshold queued for human review. Non-destructive — no data changes until HQ confirms.
Step 2 — HQ review queueHQ sees: (a) Auto-matched — proposed links to confirm; (b) Review required — low-confidence pairs; (c) Unmatched outlet items — absorb, local-exception, or deprecate; (d) Conflicts — mismatched UOM/category requiring resolution.
Step 3 — Outcome statesEach outlet item resolves to: Mapped (linked to HQ master, outlet loses edit rights), Local exception (outlet retains control, HQ-visible), or Deprecated (archived, history re-referenced to HQ item).

5.6 Data migration — existing Zeemart buyers

  • Step 1 — Inventory pass: buyer's existing market list items, inventory items, and recipe ingredients extracted and deduplicated into a candidate internal item list.
  • Step 2 — Normalisation: AI matching agent normalises names, UOMs, categories; anomalies flagged for human review.
  • Step 3 — Supplier SKU linkage: existing market list entries converted into Supplier SKU Links. Historical data retains supplier SKU reference; COGS re-computed against internal item.
  • Step 4 — Mode assignment: group assigned HQ Mode or Standalone Mode. HQ Mode groups with multiple outlets go through the reconciliation workflow.
  • Step 5 — Validation: pre-go-live validation report confirms every recipe ingredient, inventory item, and open order line resolves to a valid internal item + supplier SKU link.
Migration runs outlet-group by outlet-group — not a single bulk cutover — to limit blast radius and allow rollback per group.

5.7 Key benefits unlocked

  • Supplier flexibility: switch or add suppliers without touching recipes, inventory history, or COGS.
  • Price comparison: multiple supplier SKUs linked to one item enables side-by-side price comparison and lowest-cost fulfillment recommendations.
  • In-house items: prep items, CK outputs, own-brand goods created as internal items with no supplier SKU link.
  • Cleaner COGS: variance and COGS compute against the internal item, eliminating fragmentation from the same ingredient appearing under multiple supplier SKUs across outlets.
  • HQ governance: HQ controls the canonical item definition while suppliers compete to be the linked fulfillment source — buyer-first power structure.

6.5 ICP sanity-check rules

  • SMB tax: does this feature add complexity to self-serve onboarding or require human intervention for ICP 1? If yes, it must be optional/hidden for Standalone Mode or gated to ICP 2+.
  • Enterprise floor: does this feature require capabilities (SSO, API, multi-level approvals, audit logs) that ICP 3 treats as non-negotiable? If yes, it cannot be deprioritised below P1.
  • Mode integrity: does this feature assume HQ Mode in a way that breaks a Standalone Mode user? If yes, it must be mode-aware in its UI and data model.
  • CK assumption: does this feature assume a Central Kitchen exists? If yes, it must degrade gracefully for ICP 1 and ICP 3 groups without a CK.
  • Price-to-value for ICP 1: would an SMB at $200/month feel this justifies part of their subscription? If a feature only serves ICP 3, it should not drive up base-tier pricing.

7. Vision, Objectives & Non-Goals

7.1 Product vision

Every multi-outlet F&B group runs procurement, inventory, recipes, and cost control through Nomni Procure — with HQ defining policy once, outlets executing within guardrails, suppliers transacting natively on the platform, and Nomni agents doing the repetitive work: drafting orders, reconciling invoices, flagging variances, and surfacing decisions that need a human.

7.2 Business objectives

  • O1 — Category leadership: be the reference platform for multi-outlet procurement governance in SEA, AU, and MEA within 18 months of GA.
  • O2 — Centralised governance: HQ defines and enforces market lists, recipes, inventory standards, and POS mappings with zero manual per-outlet effort.
  • O3 — Effort reduction: reduce outlet-level time on ordering, receiving, and reconciliation by 50%+ through automation and agentic workflows.
  • O4 — Cost intelligence: give HQ live COGS, stock variance, and spend analytics trusted enough to drive weekly decisions.
  • O5 — Network effects: grow the transactional supplier network so switching away from Nomni Procure means losing supplier connectivity, not just software.
  • O6 — Data accuracy & auditability: every HQ and agent action is logged, attributable, and reversible where applicable.

7.3 Non-goals (this release)

  • NG1 — Workforce management: rostering, scheduling, and payroll are out of scope — integrate with Restoke/Tanda instead.
  • NG2 — Front-of-house: no POS, reservations, or guest-facing capabilities. We integrate with POS, we do not replace it.
  • NG3 — Accounting: no general ledger. Export and sync to accounting platforms.
  • NG4 — Backend re-platform: the Java/MongoDB core is wrapped, not rewritten.
  • NG5 — Fully autonomous purchasing: agents draft and recommend; humans approve above configured thresholds.

10. Functional Requirements

10.1 Market List Assignment

  • ML-1 — HQ can organise and manage market lists by outlet grouping (brand → region → state → outlet).
  • ML-2 — HQ can assign a market list — or individual items — to all outlets in a group or a selected subset.
  • ML-3 — Changes to an assigned market list propagate automatically to all assigned outlets, with status visible per outlet (propagated / pending / error).
  • ML-4 — Propagation never affects in-flight orders containing removed or modified items.
  • ML-5 — Scheduled propagation: HQ can stage changes to take effect at a future date/time.
  • ML-6 — Conflict surface: where an outlet-level exception exists, propagation flags rather than silently overwrites.
  • ML-7 — Nomni agent can draft market-list updates from supplier price-file changes for HQ one-click approval.

10.2 Recipe Management

  • RC-1 — HQ can create and maintain a central recipe library, including sub-recipes/prep recipes.
  • RC-2 — Pushed recipes are locked for outlet users — ingredients and quantities cannot be modified.
  • RC-3 — Full version history with timestamps and author; HQ can roll back to any prior version.
  • RC-4 — Recipes include selling price, synced bidirectionally with POS price.
  • RC-5 — Live recipe costing: ingredient cost changes recompute recipe cost and margin in real time.
  • RC-6 — Margin alerts: configurable thresholds trigger notification when price changes push a recipe below target margin, with agent-suggested responses.
  • RC-7 — Approval workflow: recipe changes can require HQ Approver sign-off before propagation.
  • RC-8 — Allergen and nutrition fields per recipe, propagated with the recipe.

10.3 Inventory Management

  • IN-1 — HQ defines a master inventory item list (name, category, UOM) and pushes it to outlets or outlet groups.
  • IN-2 — Outlets can organise HQ-defined items into their own lists; items can appear in multiple lists.
  • IN-3 — Outlets set and update par levels per item; par levels can also be HQ-templated by outlet archetype.
  • IN-4 — Items removed by HQ show downstream impact (N outlets, X recipes, Y POS mappings) and require confirmation.
  • IN-5 — Bulk upload via standardised Excel template with validation report.
  • IN-6 — Stock counts: scheduled and spot counts on mobile, variance computed against theoretical stock.
  • IN-7 — Automated variance analysis by outlet, category, and ingredient, with drill-down.
  • IN-8 — Par-based replenishment suggestions: when stock falls below par, a draft order is generated for outlet approval.
  • IN-9 — Inter-outlet stock transfers with request → approve → dispatch → receive workflow and audit trail.
  • IN-10 — Waste logging with reason codes, feeding variance and cost analytics.

10.4 POS Mapping

  • PM-1 — HQ creates and maintains a master POS mapping set and pushes it to all outlets or a subset.
  • PM-2 — Outlets cannot modify or add to POS mappings.
  • PM-3 — Variants of POS mappings can be replicated selectively for outlets with partial menu differences.
  • PM-4 — Pushing to outlets with existing mappings shows a warning and requires explicit HQ confirmation.
  • PM-5 — Mapping health monitor: unmapped POS items and stale mappings surfaced continuously per outlet.
  • PM-6 — Agent-suggested mappings: when a new POS item appears, agent proposes a mapping based on name/recipe similarity.

10.5 Procurement & Ordering (Procure-to-Pay)

The platform already runs ordering, approvals, GRN receiving, and 2/3-way match; this module adds the requisition stage, multi-level policy approvals, and spend controls.

  • PO-1 — Outlet users raise requisitions (from par suggestions, market lists, or free entry) on mobile or web.
  • PO-2 — Multi-level approval chains, configurable by amount thresholds, category, outlet, and role.
  • PO-3 — Approved requisitions become POs sent to suppliers via the network, email, or WhatsApp link fallback.
  • PO-4 — Suppliers confirm, propose substitutions/price changes, or reject; buyer-visible status throughout.
  • PO-5 — Receiving on mobile: quantity/quality confirmation against PO, partial deliveries, discrepancy capture with photos.
  • PO-6 — Live price-variance monitoring: received/invoiced price vs contracted price flagged automatically.
  • PO-7 — Spend controls: budgets per outlet/category/period with soft and hard limits enforced at requisition time.
  • PO-8 — Order consolidation: HQ or agents can consolidate demand across outlets for volume pricing.

10.6 Invoice Intelligence

PEPPOL e-invoicing exists today on both buyer and supplier sides — a compliance asset neither competitor markets. This module extends automation depth.

  • IV-1 — Invoice capture by photo, PDF, email-in, or supplier e-invoice via the network.
  • IV-2 — AI extraction of line items, quantities, prices, taxes; learning supplier formats over time.
  • IV-3 — 3-way match (PO ↔ goods received ↔ invoice) with auto pass-through for clean matches and exception queue for mismatches.
  • IV-4 — Price-change detection: invoice prices update item cost history and trigger recipe-margin recomputation.
  • IV-5 — Export/sync to accounting platforms (Xero first; extensible connector model).
  • IV-6 — Invoice archive is searchable and audit-grade (configurable retention).

10.7 Reporting & Analytics

  • RA-1 — Cross-outlet performance dashboard: consolidated HQ view with drill-down to outlet level.
  • RA-2 — Stock variance analysis: actual vs theoretical consumption (recipe data × POS sales volumes), as absolute and percentage.

10.8 Nomni AI Agent Layer

Design principle: agents execute, humans govern. Every agent action is attributable, auditable, and either approval-gated or reversible.
AgentFunction
Ordering agentDrafts replenishment orders from par levels, sales velocity, and lead times. Outlet approves with one tap. Learns from edits.
Invoice agentProcesses invoice queue end-to-end. Clean 3-way matches auto-post (configurable). Exceptions summarised with recommended resolution.
Margin agentWatches price changes; flags recipes breaching target margin with quantified options (reprice, rebuild, re-source).
Variance agentInvestigates stock variances, correlates with waste logs, transfers, and count quality; produces outlet-level explanations.
Governance agent (HQ)Drafts market-list and mapping updates from supplier price files and POS changes. HQ bulk-approves.

Guardrails (non-negotiable)

  • AG-6 — Every agent action is logged with agent identity, input data, and rationale summary.
  • AG-7 — State-changing actions above configurable thresholds require human approval; below threshold they are auto-applied but reversible (Undo window).
  • AG-8 — Agents operate only within the requesting user's permission scope.
  • AG-9 — Per-group agent enablement: HQ can switch each agent capability on/off and set autonomy thresholds.
  • AG-10 — Agent token/compute cost is monitored per group with budget alerts.

17. Conclusion

This BRD carries forward every commitment of the original HQ Module Enhancements document and extends it to a product that can lead its category. The competitive analysis is direct about the bar: Supy defines operational rigour, Restoke defines AI ambition. Nomni Procure's winning position combines both and adds what neither has — a transactional supplier network and an agent layer with enterprise-grade governance, delivered on an API-first architecture that compounds.

Three foundational decisions differentiate this release: the buyer-primary data model (§5) flips the power structure from supplier-led to buyer-led; the three ICP model (§6) ensures the product serves SMB self-serve, mid-market hybrid, and enterprise governance without conflating them; and the agent layer with hard guardrails (§10.8) makes AI a trusted execution partner rather than an experimental feature.
