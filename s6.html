Section 6

Ideal Customer Profiles

Three ICPs that define the addressable market.

Three ICPs define the addressable market for Nomni Procure at launch. Every product decision — feature prioritisation, pricing, onboarding design, agent autonomy defaults, and support model — should be sanity-checked against these profiles. Geography: Southeast Asia, Australia, and Middle East & Africa.

6.1 ICP 1 — SMB Operator

AttributeProfile
Who they areIndependent restaurant, cafe, bar, food truck, ghost kitchen, catering business, or small multi-concept operator. Owner or manager is typically the same person. 1–10 outlets, each often operating independently.
Size signal1–10 outlets. No dedicated procurement team — the owner, chef, or outlet manager handles ordering.
Operating modeStandalone Mode. Each outlet controls its own item master, supplier links, and ordering.
Current toolsSpreadsheets, WhatsApp ordering, paper GRNs, or entry-level tools. Xero/MYOB for accounting.
Primary painTime lost on manual ordering and invoice chasing; no visibility of actual vs theoretical food cost; price changes caught late.
What they buyOrdering, invoice digitisation, basic inventory and stock counts, spending reports, recipe costing, POS integration.
What they don't needHQ governance, multi-level approvals, central kitchen workflows, RFQ, complex reporting.
Price sensitivityHigh. Max ~$200/outlet/month. Must feel like it saves more than it costs within month one.
Onboarding100% self-serve. Signup, onboarding, supplier connection, and first order without any Nomni human intervention. Time-to-first-order target: same day.
MarketsAustralia (independent cafes, bars), Singapore (cloud kitchens), UAE/KSA (independent dining).

6.2 ICP 2 — F&B Group (Mid-Market)

AttributeProfile
Who they areA growing F&B group with multiple outlets under one or more brands, with a Central Kitchen (CK) supplying some or all outlets. Outlets buy from both external suppliers and the CK.
Size signal~10–50 outlets. One or more brands. At least one Central Kitchen entity. May include franchise and company-owned outlets.
Operating modeHybrid — HQ Mode + Local Exception. HQ/Ops controls the item master and CK supply. Outlets order from both external suppliers and the CK.
Central KitchenCK is a first-class entity in Nomni Supply Hub. Billing: cost-price or mark-up for P&L separation.
Key rolesOps/Procurement person manages the CK and group settings. Outlet managers handle day-to-day ordering and receiving.
Primary painCoordinating CK supply with outlet demand; inconsistent item naming making COGS unreliable; no single view of ordered vs received vs produced vs sold.
What they buyFull procure-to-pay, CK internal ordering portal, inventory and variance analytics, recipe costing with live margins, approval workflows.
OnboardingGuided remote setup — one Nomni implementation touchpoint to configure the CK entity, reconcile catalogues, and set approval policies.
MarketsSingapore (multi-concept groups), Malaysia, Thailand, Australia (regional chains), UAE.

6.3 ICP 3 — Large F&B Enterprise

AttributeProfile
Who they areA large F&B enterprise operating 50–300+ outlets. Dedicated procurement or supply chain team at HQ. May include franchise and company-owned models.
Size signal50–300+ outlets. HQ team of 3–10+ people. May span multiple countries.
Operating modeHQ Mode — fully governed. HQ owns and controls item master, recipes, market lists, POS mappings, and approval policies. Outlets execute within HQ guardrails.
Franchise specificsHQ defines approved supplier list and contracted pricing. Franchisees order only from approved suppliers at HQ-negotiated prices. Compliance visible to HQ in real time.
Current toolsMix of ERP (SAP, Oracle), legacy procurement system, and spreadsheets for outlet-level operations.
Primary painInconsistent outlet compliance; no real-time COGS visibility; invoice reconciliation at scale is a major finance overhead; onboarding new franchise partners is slow.
What they buyFull HQ governance suite, procure-to-pay at scale, invoice intelligence, cross-outlet analytics, supplier network and RFQ, SSO/MFA, public API for ERP integration, dedicated SLA.
OnboardingEnterprise sales and implementation project. API integration with existing ERP. Dedicated AM and SLA-backed support.
Price sensitivityLow — pays for compliance, auditability, and scale.
MarketsAustralia (national QSR and casual dining chains), Singapore (regional HQ of SEA franchise groups), Saudi Arabia/UAE (large MEA franchise operators).

6.5 ICP sanity-check rules

  • SMB tax: does this feature add complexity to self-serve onboarding or require human intervention for ICP 1? If yes, it must be optional/hidden for Standalone Mode or gated to ICP 2+.
  • Enterprise floor: does this feature require capabilities (SSO, API, multi-level approvals, audit logs) that ICP 3 treats as non-negotiable? If yes, it cannot be deprioritised below P1.
  • Mode integrity: does this feature assume HQ Mode in a way that breaks a Standalone Mode user? If yes, it must be mode-aware in its UI and data model.
  • CK assumption: does this feature assume a Central Kitchen exists? If yes, it must degrade gracefully for ICP 1 and ICP 3 groups without a CK.
  • Price-to-value for ICP 1: would an SMB at $200/month feel this justifies part of their subscription? If a feature only serves ICP 3, it should not drive up base-tier pricing.
